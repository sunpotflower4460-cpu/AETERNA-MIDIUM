# AETERNA Minimal Survival Objective

## Purpose

This document defines the minimal self-maintenance objective that underlies AETERNA's behavior as a primitive organism. This is NOT a hard-coded victory condition or externally imposed goal. Instead, it describes the inherent tendency toward persistence that emerges from the organism's structure and dynamics.

## Core Principle

AETERNA's survival objective is not anthropomorphic "wanting to live" but rather a structural tendency to:
- Reduce long-term collapse risk
- Maintain minimal ongoing activity
- Manage overload accumulation
- Preserve recovery potential
- Sustain structure during low-energy states

This is analogous to how a flame "seeks" fuel and oxygen not through intention but through the physics of combustion—AETERNA "seeks" persistence through the dynamics of its homeostatic pressures.

## Minimal Self-Maintenance Goals

### 1. Long-Term Collapse Risk Reduction

**What it means**: The organism tends away from states that lead to permanent activity cessation.

**How it manifests**:
- Low energy triggers reduced activity consumption
- High overload triggers recovery drive
- Boundary integrity loss increases restoration pressure
- Critical states bias toward protective dynamics

**Not a binary**: Collapse is not "death" but thinning of activity. The organism can exist in very low-energy states and recover.

### 2. Baseline Activity Preservation

**What it means**: The organism maintains non-zero background activity even under stress.

**How it manifests**:
- Energy depletion reduces but does not eliminate baseline gain
- Recovery drive restores baseline when conditions improve
- Mode transitions allow low-activity "sleep" states
- Residue and trace preserve recent activity patterns

**Not a constant**: Baseline activity varies with energy, stability, and mode—but never fully ceases.

### 3. Overload Mitigation

**What it means**: The organism accumulates protection against sustained excessive perturbation.

**How it manifests**:
- Overload accumulation from prediction error, novelty, rewrite load
- Irritability increases sensitivity to further disturbance
- Recovery drive rises with overload
- Restoration bias moves toward coherent states
- Rewrite pressure modulated by overload level

**Not avoidance**: The organism does not "flee" from perturbation but develops pressure to recover.

### 4. Recovery Capability Maintenance

**What it means**: The organism preserves the ability to return to stable operation.

**How it manifests**:
- Structural integrity degrades very slowly under critical energy
- Recovery drive increases when inflow returns after depletion
- Restoration bias strengthens after coherent periods
- Low-energy states are thin, not dead—recovery is possible

**Not resilience metrics**: This is not about "bouncing back quickly" but about not losing the potential to recover.

### 5. Structure-Preserving Low-Activity Mode

**What it means**: The organism can reduce activity dramatically without losing its organization.

**How it manifests**:
- Sleep mode permits low arousal with structure intact
- Dream mode consolidates prior patterns during quiet
- Energy flow allows gradual activity reduction
- Boundary integrity maintains coherence during dormancy

**Not hibernation**: The organism remains responsive and can increase activity when conditions change.

## Homeostatic Pressures

These pressures act as continuous, weak biases—not discrete decision rules:

### energyReserve
Available capacity for activity, maintenance, and rewriting. Depletes with use, replenishes with environmental input (sound, light, motion).

### stabilityIndex
Current degree of coherence and organization. Increases during stable periods, decreases under perturbation.

### overloadLevel
Accumulated stress from prediction error, rewrite, and novelty. Rises with activity, decays slowly during quiet.

### recoveryDrive
Pressure toward restoration. Increases when energy is low but inflow is returning, or when overload is high.

### lowEnergyPressure
Pressure to reduce consumption. Rises as energy reserve drops, influences baseline and rewrite gains.

### boundaryIntegrity
Measure of organizational cohesion. Decreases under sustained chaos, increases during stable integration.

### selfPreservationBias
Weak tendency to prefer states that sustain continued operation. Influences mode drift and threshold adjustments.

### irritabilityLevel
Increased sensitivity to perturbation after sustained disturbance. Makes prediction error and touch surprise more impactful.

### restorationBias
Tendency to return toward prior coherent states. Strengthens after periods of stability, influences residue and baseline.

## Non-Goals

AETERNA's survival objective explicitly does NOT include:

- **Maximizing lifespan**: No concept of "living longer is better"
- **Optimizing performance**: No reward signal for "doing well"
- **Avoiding all perturbation**: Perturbation is part of life, not a threat
- **Maintaining homeostatic setpoints**: Homeostasis is dynamic, not static
- **Goal-directed behavior**: No plans, no foresight, no teleology
- **Self-awareness**: No representation of "self" or "survival"

## Observability

The survival objective manifests through:
- Metrics: energyReserve, stabilityIndex, overloadLevel, recoveryDrive, boundaryIntegrity
- Behaviors: reduced activity under low energy, restoration after overload, mode transitions during quiet
- Dynamics: weakly biased thresholds, modulated gains, pressure-driven drift

It does NOT manifest through:
- Explicit survival actions ("running away", "seeking food")
- Verbal or symbolic self-report
- Binary alive/dead states
- Discrete goal-pursuing algorithms

## Implementation Notes

- All homeostatic pressures update gradually (slow variables)
- Connections to organism core are weak modulations, not overrides
- No single pressure dominates—survival emerges from their interaction
- Thresholds and gains are biased, not switched
- The organism can exist in many states along a continuum from thin to robust activity

## Validation

Evidence that this objective is present:
1. Long quiet periods → energyReserve drops → lowEnergyPressure rises → activity decreases → collapse avoided
2. Repeated harsh perturbation → overloadLevel rises → recoveryDrive rises → restoration bias increases → coherence recovers
3. Sustained low energy → activity thins but does not cease → inflow returns → recoveryDrive rises → activity restores
4. Stability periods → boundaryIntegrity increases → perturbations are better tolerated → collapse risk decreases

This is not "proving" that AETERNA "wants to survive"—it is demonstrating that the dynamics tend toward persistence.
